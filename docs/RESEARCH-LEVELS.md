# CEFR A1–B2 ladder and practice-exam research for Ohrly

Research date: 9 November 2026. Scope: adult/general German and English unless stated. This continues the original A1 sequences in `RESEARCH.md` §§2–3. All proposed curricula, passage lengths and Ohrly tests below are original designs, not copied exam content. `UNVERIFIED` means a primary source did not establish the point.

## 1. Exam ladder

The CEFR is a framework of action-oriented “can-do” descriptors across reception, production, interaction and mediation, not a single syllabus or test; a learner can have an uneven profile ([Council of Europe overview](https://www.coe.int/en/web/portfolio/the-common-european-framework-of-reference-for-languages-learning-teaching-assessment-cefr-), [2020 Companion Volume](https://rm.coe.int/cefr-companion-volume-with-new-descriptors-2020/16809ea0d4)). Therefore “at B1” below means the named examination's standard, not that every skill is necessarily identical.

### German: Goethe-Zertifikat

**Goethe-Zertifikat A1: Start Deutsch 1 (adult).** The adult A1 name is *Start Deutsch 1*; it was jointly developed with telc. Goethe's official specification gives four equally weighted skills ([test description](https://www.goethe.de/pro/relaunch/prf/sk/Pruefungsziele_Testbeschreibung_A1_SD1.pdf), [model set](https://www.goethe.de/pro/relaunch/prf/materialien/A1_sd1/sd_1_modellsatz.pdf)).

| Paper | Parts / tasks | Formats | Time |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Listening | 3 / 15 items | short conversations, phone/PA messages; 3-option choice and true/false | ~20 min |
| Reading | 3 / 15 items | short notes, notices, ads, signs; true/false and 3-option choice | 25 min |
| Writing | 2 / 2 tasks | complete a form; ~30-word personal message | 20 min |
| Speaking | 3 / 3 task families | introduce self; ask/answer from cards; make/respond to requests (group, no preparation) | 15 min |

Pass: 60/100 overall after taking every section; written skills total 75 and speaking 25. It is not modular and is retaken as a whole ([2025 regulations](https://www.goethe.de/pro/relaunch/prf/sl/Durchfuehrungsbestimmungen_A1_Start_Deutsch_1.pdf)).

**Goethe-Zertifikat A2.** ([official exam page](https://www.goethe.de/ins/de/en/prf/gzsd2.cfm), [official online model](https://bfu.goethe.de/a2_mod_2MX5/lesen.php))

| Paper | Parts / tasks | Formats | Time |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Reading | 4 / 20 items | article and email MCQ; directory lookup; people-to-ad matching | 30 min |
| Listening | 4 / 20 items | short radio/phone/PA texts and conversations; MCQ/matching | 30 min |
| Writing | 2 / 2 tasks | 20–30-word SMS; 30–40-word email, each covering 3 points | 30 min |
| Speaking | 3 / 3 task families | personal questions; short account of own life; plan/negotiate with partner | 15 min |

Pass: 60/100 overall, with at least 45/75 in the combined written exam and 15/25 speaking; all sections required, not separately certifiable ([official result rules](https://www.goethe.de/de/spr/prf/pes/paa2.html)).

**Goethe-Zertifikat B1.** Each module is independently taken/certified ([official page](https://www.goethe.de/ins/de/en/prf/gzb1.cfm), [model set](https://www.goethe.de/ins/de/de/prf/gzb1/ueb.html)).

| Module | Parts / tasks | Formats | Time |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Reading | 5 / 30 items | blog true/false; press MCQ; situation–ad matching; opinions yes/no; rules MCQ | 65 min |
| Listening | 4 / 30 items | announcements, short talks, conversation, radio discussion; T/F, MCQ, speaker matching | 40 min |
| Writing | 3 / 3 tasks | personal email, opinion/forum contribution, short formal email | 60 min |
| Speaking | 3 / 3 task families | plan together; individual presentation; react to questions/partner | 15 min (plus preparation) |

Pass: each module has 100 points and passes independently at 60; there is no compensating overall average ([official results](https://www.goethe.de/en/spr/prf/pes/pab1.html)).

**Goethe-Zertifikat B2.** Each module is independently taken/certified ([official page](https://www.goethe.de/ins/de/en/prf/gzb2.cfm), [model set](https://www.goethe.de/pro/relaunch/prf/materialien/B2/b2_modellsatz_erwachsene.pdf)).

| Module | Parts / tasks | Formats | Time |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Reading | 5 / 30 items | forum matching; article/argument MCQ; gapped text; viewpoint matching; rules/lexicogrammar | 65 min |
| Listening | 4 / 30 items | short statements, interview, conversation, lecture; T/F, MCQ, matching | ~40 min |
| Writing | 2 / 2 tasks | ~150-word forum argument; ~100-word formal professional message | 75 min |
| Speaking | 2 / 2 tasks | ~4-minute presentation plus questions; paired discussion | 15 min + 15 min preparation |

Pass: 60/100 in each module independently ([Goethe modular policy](https://www.goethe.de/de/spr/prf.html), [B2 regulations/model criteria](https://bfu.goethe.de/b2_mod_2MX6/schreiben.php)).

### English: Cambridge English Qualifications and IELTS context

There is no general-adult Cambridge qualification targeted at A1. Cambridge's named A1 examination is **A1 Movers**, designed for primary/lower-secondary learners; it has no pass/fail ([YLE handbook](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/Images/357180-starters-movers-and-flyers-handbook-for-teachers-2024.pdf)). Adults can receive an A1 result by scoring 100–119 on A2 Key, but A2 Key is targeted at A2, not an A1 exam ([A2 Key handbook](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/Images/610842-a2-key-handbook-for-teachers.pdf)).

**Cambridge A1 Movers (young learners only).**

| Paper | Parts / tasks | Formats | Time |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Listening | 5 / 25 questions | picture/name matching, form completion, picture MCQ, colouring/instruction following | ~25 min |
| Reading & Writing | 6 / 35 questions | definitions, dialogue response, cloze/title, grammar cloze, story completion, picture sentences | 30 min |
| Speaking | 4 / 4 tasks | spot differences; continue picture story; odd-one-out/reason; personal questions | 5–7 min |

There is no pass mark: each component receives 1–5 shields and every completing child receives a certificate ([official format](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/exams-and-tests/qualifications/young-learners/paper/movers/format/)). Ohrly should call its adult A1 endpoint an “Ohrly A1 practice check,” not “Cambridge A1.”

**Cambridge A2 Key.**

| Paper | Parts / tasks | Formats | Time / weight |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Reading & Writing | 7 / 32 questions | notices MCQ; multiple matching; long-text MCQ; lexical/open cloze; ≥25-word email; ≥35-word picture story | 60 min / 50% |
| Listening | 5 / 25 questions | picture MCQ, note completion, dialogue MCQ, gist MCQ, matching | ~30 min / 25% |
| Speaking | 2 / 2 tasks | interview; paired likes/dislikes discussion with reasons | 8–10 min / 25% |

Pass at target level: overall Cambridge English Scale 120–139 (A2); 140–150 reports B1, 100–119 reports A1. Components are not passed separately and weak performance can be compensated in the overall result ([official format](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/exams-and-tests/qualifications/key/format/), [result rules](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/Images/557749-a2-key-information-for-candidates.pdf)).

**Cambridge B1 Preliminary.**

| Paper | Parts / tasks | Formats | Time / weight |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Reading | 6 / 32 questions | notices MCQ; matching; long-text MCQ; gapped text; multiple/open cloze | 45 min / 25% |
| Writing | 2 / 2 tasks | ~100-word email; ~100-word article or story | 45 min / 25% |
| Listening | 4 / 25 questions | visual MCQ, gist MCQ, gap fill, interview/detail MCQ | ~30 min / 25% |
| Speaking | 4 / 4 tasks | interview; photo monologue; collaborative discussion; general conversation | 10–12 min per pair / 25% |

Pass at target level: overall scale 140–159; 160–170 reports B2, 120–139 reports A2. Four component scores are averaged; no separate paper pass ([official format](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/exams-and-tests/qualifications/preliminary/format/), [official results table](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/exams-and-tests/qualifications/results/)).

**Cambridge B2 First.**

| Paper | Parts / tasks | Formats | Time / weight |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Reading & Use of English | 7 / 52 questions | multiple/open cloze, word formation, key-word transform, MCQ, gapped text, matching | 75 min / Reading 20% + Use 20% |
| Writing | 2 / 2 tasks | 140–190-word compulsory essay; one 140–190-word genre choice | 80 min / 20% |
| Listening | 4 / 30 questions | short-extract MCQ, sentence completion, matching, long-text MCQ | ~40 min / 20% |
| Speaking | 4 / 4 tasks | interview; photo comparison/long turn; collaborative task; discussion | 14 min per pair / 20% |

Pass at target level: average of Reading, Use of English, Writing, Listening and Speaking scale scores 160–179; 180–190 reports C1, 140–159 reports B1. Papers are not passed separately ([official handbook](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/Images/167791-b2-first-handbook.pdf)).

**IELTS is not a level-ending exam.** It spans a proficiency continuum and has no universal pass mark; institutions set requirements. Its overall band is the four skill bands averaged and rounded to the nearest half band ([official scoring](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/your-results/ielts-scoring-in-detail)).

| IELTS paper | Parts / tasks | Formats | Time |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Listening | 4 / 40 questions | MCQ, matching, plans/diagrams, form/note/table/summary/sentence completion, short answer | ~30 min |
| Reading | 3 / 40 questions | MCQ, T/F/NG, matching, headings, completion, short answer | 60 min |
| Writing | 2 / 2 tasks | Academic visual description or General letter (≥150); essay (≥250) | 60 min |
| Speaking | 3 / 3 tasks | interview; prepared long turn; abstract follow-up discussion | 11–14 min |

Official comparison is approximate, not one-to-one: B1 ≈ 4.0–5.0 and B2 ≈ 5.5–6.5; band 5 is explicitly borderline in the mapping. A1/A2 IELTS equivalents are not published in the official comparison and are therefore `UNVERIFIED`—show “not applicable,” not invented bands ([IELTS comparison](https://ielts.org/news-and-insights/finding-the-right-english-proficiency-test-for-you), [mapping caveats](https://ielts.org/organisations/ielts-for-organisations/compare-ielts/ielts-and-the-cefr)).

## 2. How long a level takes, and honest scope

These are cumulative from complete beginner, not promises or “time in the app.”

| Target | German evidence | English evidence |
|---|---:|---:|
| A1 | 80–200 lessons × 45 min = 60–150 hours | 90–100 guided hours |
| A2 | 200–350 × 45 min = 150–262.5 hours | 180–200 guided hours |
| B1 | 350–650 × 45 min = 262.5–487.5 hours | 350–400 guided hours |
| B2 | 600–800 × 45 min = 450–600 hours | 500–600 guided hours |

German figures are Goethe's experience-based exam-readiness ranges ([A1](https://www.goethe.de/ins/de/en/prf/gzsd1.cfm), [A2](https://www.goethe.de/ins/de/en/prf/gzsd2.cfm), [B1 listing](https://www.goethe.de/ins/hon/cn/m/spr/exams/ogf.html), [B2](https://www.goethe.de/ins/de/de/prf/gzb2.cfm)); English figures are Cambridge's approximate cumulative guided-learning hours and explicitly vary with background, intensity and outside exposure ([Cambridge support](https://support.cambridgeenglish.org/hc/en-gb/articles/202838506-Guided-learning-hours)).

Ohrly can reliably auto-assess selected-response reading/listening, spelling-constrained short answers, vocabulary and controlled grammar against authored answer sets. It can time tasks and report subskill evidence. Without a trained human marker and live interlocutor it cannot validly certify open writing quality, discourse/pragmatics, pronunciation/intelligibility, spontaneous interaction or mediation. Browser speech recognition is a rehearsal aid, not an examiner.

Promise wording: **“Complete Ohrly's structured A1–B2 curriculum and practise exam-style tasks. Progress depends on your starting point, study time and real-world use; Ohrly completion is not a CEFR certificate or a guarantee of passing an external exam.”** Learners seeking an exam must also speak weekly with a teacher/partner, obtain rubric-based human feedback on timed writing, listen to varied speakers/accents at natural speed, read beyond graded app text, rehearse paired speaking and complete official sample tests under official timing.

## 3. German syllabus (English speakers): A2, B1, B2

The sequences operationalise CEFR descriptors and the Goethe A2/B1 lexical inventories rather than reproducing course text. Goethe gives about 1,300 lexical units at A2 and about 2,400 at B1 ([A2 list](https://www.goethe.de/pro/relaunch/prf/en/Goethe-Zertifikat_A2_Wortliste.pdf), [B1 basis](https://www.goethe.de/resources/files/pdf209/olena-beketova_wortschatz-digital.pdf)); Goethe publishes no comparable official B2 word list found in this research (`UNVERIFIED`). Targets below are 8–12 retrieval anchors per unit, not the whole receptive syllabus. Teach every German noun with article and plural.

### German A2 — 6 chapters × 5 units

| Ch/U | Unit and can-do | Grammar | 10 targets | Topic |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | **What I did** — report completed daily actions | Perfekt: *haben* + regular participle | gestern; machen; gemacht; lernen; gelernt; arbeiten; gearbeitet; kaufen; gekauft; erzählen | routines |
| 1.2 | **A trip away** — recount movement and arrival | Perfekt: *sein*; inseparable/separable participles | fahren; gefahren; fliegen; geflogen; ankommen; angekommen; bleiben; geblieben; die Reise; unterwegs | travel |
| 1.3 | **Back then** — state past situations and possession | Präteritum of *sein/haben* | war; waren; hatte; hatten; früher; damals; das Kind; die Schule; jung; erinnern | childhood |
| 1.4 | **Who gets what?** — give and explain recipients | dative articles/pronouns; *geben/helfen* | mir; dir; ihm; ihr; uns; geben; helfen; schenken; das Geschenk; der Kollege | gifts |
| 1.5 | **Weekend story** — sequence a short past account | Perfekt review; *zuerst/dann/danach* | zuerst; danach; plötzlich; schließlich; passieren; verlieren; finden; zurückkommen; das Erlebnis; zum Glück | narrative |
| 2.1 | **At home** — locate objects precisely | two-way prepositions + dative (*wo?*) | an; auf; hinter; in; neben; über; unter; vor; zwischen; die Wand | home |
| 2.2 | **Move it there** — say where objects go | two-way prepositions + accusative (*wohin?*) | stellen; legen; hängen; setzen; das Regal; der Boden; die Ecke; hinein; hinaus; gegenüber | moving |
| 2.3 | **Around the region** — describe routes and sources | dative prepositions *aus/bei/mit/nach/seit/von/zu* | aus; bei; mit; nach; seit; von; zu; die Richtung; der Weg; entlang | directions |
| 2.4 | **Better and best** — compare choices | comparative/superlative; *als/wie* | besser; lieber; größer; günstiger; am besten; als; wie; vergleichen; der Vorteil; der Nachteil | choices |
| 2.5 | **The right one** — describe and select things | adjective endings after definite/indefinite articles, nom/acc | bequem; modern; praktisch; hell; dunkel; günstig; teuer; der Mantel; das Möbelstück; auswählen | shopping/home |
| 3.1 | **Because…** — give everyday reasons | subordinate clause with *weil* | weil; deshalb; müde; verpassen; absagen; der Grund; pünktlich; beschäftigt; regnen; hoffen | reasons |
| 3.2 | **I think that…** — report thoughts and information | *dass* clauses | dass; glauben; meinen; wissen; hoffen; die Nachricht; stimmen; sicher; wahrscheinlich; behaupten | opinions |
| 3.3 | **If and whenever** — state conditions/routines | *wenn* clauses | wenn; falls; jedes Mal; möglich; die Bedingung; planen; verschieben; entscheiden; Bescheid sagen; rechtzeitig | arrangements |
| 3.4 | **Look after yourself** — describe self-care | reflexive verbs/pronouns | sich fühlen; sich ausruhen; sich waschen; sich anziehen; sich bewegen; sich entspannen; gesund; der Stress; die Ruhe; genug | health |
| 3.5 | **Could you?** — make polite requests and wishes | *würde/könnte* as Konjunktiv II chunks | könnte; würde; hätte gern; dürfte; freundlich; höflich; die Bitte; empfehlen; reservieren; möglich | service |
| 4.1 | **At the doctor** — explain symptoms and advice | dative body constructions; modal advice | der Hals; der Rücken; husten; die Erkältung; untersuchen; das Rezept; die Tablette; weh tun; sollen; vermeiden | health care |
| 4.2 | **Workday** — describe duties and workplace | modal verbs in Präteritum | musste; konnte; wollte; durfte; sollte; die Aufgabe; die Schicht; erledigen; zuständig; anstrengend | work |
| 4.3 | **Learning paths** — discuss education history | *seit/vor* + dative; temporal clauses | die Ausbildung; der Abschluss; die Prüfung; bestehen; durchfallen; seit; vor; während; der Kurs; sich bewerben | education |
| 4.4 | **Digital life** — explain simple tech problems | separable/inseparable verbs; imperative review | herunterladen; hochladen; einschalten; ausschalten; speichern; löschen; das Passwort; die Datei; die Verbindung; funktionieren | technology |
| 4.5 | **Formal messages** — request and respond appropriately | formal register; indirect questions with *ob* | ob; mitteilen; sich erkundigen; bestätigen; die Auskunft; die Anfrage; der Betreff; geehrt; freundlich; erreichbar | correspondence |
| 5.1 | **Neighbourhood rules** — understand obligations | *man*; passive-like *sein + zu* chunks; modals | die Hausordnung; erlaubt; verboten; beachten; Rücksicht nehmen; der Lärm; die Mülltonne; der Nachbar; gemeinsam; sauber | community |
| 5.2 | **News and media** — summarise a simple report | indirect information with *dass*; source phrases | die Meldung; berichten; die Zeitung; der Sender; die Sendung; aktuell; öffentlich; angeblich; laut; zusammenfassen | media |
| 5.3 | **Nature choices** — discuss practical sustainability | purpose with *damit*; *um…zu* introduction | die Umwelt; sparen; trennen; vermeiden; wiederverwenden; der Abfall; die Energie; damit; umweltfreundlich; schützen | environment |
| 5.4 | **Celebrations** — describe customs and invitations | adjective endings review; reciprocal *einander* | das Fest; der Brauch; feiern; gratulieren; einander; die Hochzeit; die Einladung; typisch; traditionell; gemeinsam | culture |
| 5.5 | **People who help** — describe people and roles | simple relative clauses nom (*der/die/das*) | der Mensch; die Person; der Verein; freiwillig; unterstützen; betreuen; derjenige; hilfreich; engagiert; teilnehmen | volunteering |
| 6.1 | **Travel problem** — complain and seek a solution | polite KII; causal connectors | sich beschweren; die Verspätung; ausfallen; umsteigen; die Erstattung; ersetzen; trotzdem; wegen; könnte; lösen | transport |
| 6.2 | **Rent a room** — understand an ad and negotiate | adjective endings; dative/accusative review | die Miete; die Kaution; möbliert; verfügbar; besichtigen; der Vermieter; einziehen; ruhig; zentral; inklusive | housing |
| 6.3 | **Plans ahead** — state intentions and predictions | present for future; *werden* introduction | werden; vorhaben; vermutlich; demnächst; die Zukunft; das Ziel; erreichen; sich ändern; planen; hoffen | future |
| 6.4 | **A2 writing lab** — produce short functional messages | connectors *und/aber/denn/deshalb/trotzdem* | außerdem; aber; denn; deshalb; trotzdem; entschuldigen; informieren; vorschlagen; absagen; antworten | exam writing |
| 6.5 | **A2 task circuit** — handle routine reading/listening/speaking tasks | cumulative A2 review | auswählen; zuordnen; die Aussage; das Detail; verhandeln; vereinbaren; begründen; nachfragen; korrigieren; überprüfen | exam skills |

### German B1 — 6 chapters × 5 units

| Ch/U | Unit and can-do | Grammar | 10 targets | Topic |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | **Life story** — narrate stages and turning points | Präteritum of frequent verbs; Perfekt contrast | wurde; ging; kam; begann; fand; zog; entschied; die Kindheit; der Wendepunkt; aufwachsen | biography |
| 1.2 | **Before that** — order past events clearly | Plusquamperfekt; *bevor/nachdem* | bevor; nachdem; bereits; vorher; nachher; verlassen; abschließen; entdecken; die Erfahrung; inzwischen | narrative time |
| 1.3 | **Detailed people** — describe people precisely | full adjective endings, all cases | zuverlässig; geduldig; neugierig; selbstständig; aufmerksam; der Charakter; die Eigenschaft; wirken; sich verhalten; schätzen | relationships |
| 1.4 | **People I know** — add identifying information | relative clauses nom/acc/dat | dessen; deren; dem; denen; die Bekannte; der Nachbar; vorstellen; unterstützen; gemeinsam; kennenlernen | social life |
| 1.5 | **What would you do?** — discuss unreal wishes | Konjunktiv II *wäre/hätte/würde* | wäre; hätte; würde; an deiner Stelle; träumen; sich wünschen; verändern; möglich; die Entscheidung; bereuen | choices |
| 2.1 | **Work applications** — present skills formally | infinitive with *zu*; *ohne/anstatt…zu* | sich bewerben; der Lebenslauf; die Stelle; die Fähigkeit; geeignet; einstellen; kündigen; zu; ohne; anstatt | employment |
| 2.2 | **At the office** — describe processes | present passive *werden + Partizip II* | bearbeiten; liefern; prüfen; herstellen; versenden; genehmigen; der Auftrag; die Abteilung; zuständig; erledigt | work process |

## 5. Reading and listening content by level

Official formats establish genres and task families, but generally do **not** impose one fixed word count for every input text. Goethe names A1 notes/ads/signs, A2 short articles/emails/ads/boards, B1 blogs/emails/press/ads/instructions and B2 forums/press/commentary/regulations ([A1](https://www.goethe.de/ins/de/en/prf/gzsd1.cfm), [A2](https://www.goethe.de/ins/de/en/prf/gzsd2.cfm), [B1](https://www.goethe.de/ins/de/en/prf/gzb1.cfm), [B2](https://www.goethe.de/ins/de/en/prf/gzb2.cfm)). Cambridge likewise moves from A2 signs/brochures/simple press through B1 notices and everyday articles to B2 fiction/non-fiction, journals, newspapers and magazines ([A2 format](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/exams-and-tests/qualifications/key/format/), [B1 format](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/exams-and-tests/qualifications/preliminary/format/), [B2 handbook](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/Images/167791-b2-first-handbook.pdf)). Exact per-text exam word limits are therefore `UNVERIFIED`; do not present the authored ranges below as official limits.

| Level | Evidence from official models | Ohrly authored input **per unit** (design decision) | Auto-marked questions |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | micro-notes, forms, notices, signs, short personal message; slow clear conversations/phone/PA ([Goethe model](https://bfu.goethe.de/a1_sd1/lesen.php)) | 3 microtexts of 5–25 words + 1 passage of 50–80; 2 recordings of 20–45 sec | 4–6 per mode: picture/meaning choice, T/F, detail, form field, notice match |

Use one reading and one listening item for first exposure and the rest for spaced retrieval. At B1/B2 include more than one speaker and standard regional accents, but never use accent identification as the scored construct. Every question needs one defensible key, source-span annotation and distractor rationale; open paraphrases belong in self-check, not brittle exact-string grading.

## 6. Client-only practice exam design

The test should sample the real exams' skill areas and interaction patterns without copying prompts, visual layouts or recordings. Randomise from multiple original forms, lock answers only within a timed section, permit accessibility pauses only in a clearly labelled untimed mode, and keep results locally.

| Level | Auto-scored core (original items) | Timing | Ohrly readiness rule (not an external pass mark) | Unscored production |
|---|---|---:|---|---|
| A1 | Reading 15; listening 15; language-in-context 10 | 55 min | ≥24/40 overall and ≥8/15 in each receptive skill | 30-word message; 1-minute introduction + 2 request/response cards |
| A2 | Reading 20; listening 20; language-in-context 10 | 75 min | ≥30/50 overall and ≥10/20 reading and listening | 20–30-word note + 30–40-word email; personal turn + paired-plan simulation |
| B1 | Reading 30; listening 30; language-in-context 20 | 120 min | ≥48/80 overall and ≥15/30 reading and listening | two timed texts (email/opinion); 3-minute talk + planning role-play |
| B2 | Reading 30; listening 30; language-in-context 20 | 130 min | ≥48/80 overall and ≥15/30 reading and listening | 150-word argument + 100-word formal message; 4-minute talk + discussion |

These 60% thresholds are an Ohrly product decision chosen for understandable consistency with Goethe's 60% module standard, not a score conversion or prediction ([Goethe B1 result rule](https://www.goethe.de/en/spr/prf/pes/pab1.html)). Show an auto-score only for the core. For writing, show the relevant official-style dimensions in original wording—task coverage, organisation/cohesion, range, accuracy and register—then reveal one original model and a checklist after submission; never algorithmically label the prose A2/B1/B2. For speaking, let learners record, replay and tick intelligibility, completion, organisation, range and interaction prompts; speech-to-text may highlight missing key content but must not award a CEFR score. Recommend a teacher/examiner-rated sample before booking.

Button/short-card wording: **“Practice target met”** / **“Keep practising”**—never “Passed A2,” “exam ready,” “CEFR certified,” or an IELTS-band prediction.

## 7. Decisions and vocabulary targets

1. Keep the four-level ladder and the existing 30-unit/level rhythm; treat can-dos—not grammar coverage alone—as completion criteria, consistent with CEFR profiles ([CEFR descriptors](https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/cefr-descriptors)).
2. Target these **cumulative course lexical entries/senses** in each direction: A1 **650**, A2 **1,300**, B1 **2,400**, B2 **4,000**. German A1–B1 anchors reflect Goethe's published inventories; B2 4,000 and all English totals are Ohrly scope decisions, **not official CEFR/Cambridge vocabulary counts** ([Goethe A2 list](https://www.goethe.de/pro/relaunch/prf/en/Goethe-Zertifikat_A2_Wortliste.pdf), [Goethe B1](https://www.goethe.de/resources/files/pdf209/olena-beketova_wortschatz-digital.pdf), [English Profile](https://englishprofile.org/?menu=english-vocabulary-profile)). Count a distinct English sense/phrase and a German lemma with article/plural/principal forms as one authored entry; separately track receptive vs productive mastery.
3. Gate the level-ending check on coverage and spaced retrieval, not time spent. Report reading/listening/language evidence separately and leave writing/speaking visibly unscored until a human evaluates them.
4. Support both Goethe-leaning German and Cambridge-leaning English practice, with telc format familiarisation as optional variants. Mention IELTS only as B1/B2 context; do not build an “IELTS A1/A2” track.
5. Commission multiple native-speaker voices, human review of every answer key and rubric-calibrated external marking pilots before making any exam-readiness claim. Link learners to the named providers' current official model papers because formats can change.
