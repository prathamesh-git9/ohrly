# Research job: CEFR levels and exams for Ohrly

Ohrly is a free, original language-learning website, teaching German to English speakers (en-de) and
English to German speakers (de-en). A1 is being written already; read docs/RESEARCH.md sections 2 and 3
so you continue from it. We now want a ladder A1, A2, B1, B2, with a practice exam ending each level.

Use web search. Prefer primary sources: Goethe-Institut, telc, Cambridge English, IELTS, Council of Europe
CEFR documents, official word lists and handbooks. Every non-obvious claim gets a URL. Mark anything
you could not verify as UNVERIFIED. Do not guess numbers. Do not copy any exam question or course text.

Write exactly one file: docs/RESEARCH-LEVELS.md, at most about 450 lines. Do not edit anything else.
Do not commit.

## 1. The exams
For each level A1, A2, B1, B2 and each direction, name the standard exams:
- German: Goethe-Zertifikat (Start Deutsch 1 / A1, A2, B1, B2) and telc Deutsch.
- English: Cambridge (A2 Key, B1 Preliminary, B2 First), plus IELTS band equivalents, and what exists at A1.
For each exam give a table: papers/modules (reading, listening, writing, speaking), number of parts and
tasks, task formats, time per paper, and the pass mark and how it is calculated. Note where modules are
passed separately. Cite the official handbook or model-paper page.

## 2. How long a level takes
Official or well-evidenced guided learning hours per level for each language, and what this implies
about what a self-study website alone can honestly promise. Which skills can a website without a teacher
or human marker really assess? Say what a learner must also do outside the app to pass.

## 3. Syllabus A2, B1, B2 for German (for English speakers)
Original sequence: for each level, 6 chapters of 5 units (30 units per level). Each unit: title, can-do
statement, grammar point, 8-12 target words, and topic. Based on Goethe word lists and CEFR descriptors.
Must cover at least: Perfekt and Praeteritum, dative, two-way prepositions, adjective endings, comparison,
subordinate clauses (weil, dass, wenn), reflexive verbs, Konjunktiv II, relative clauses, passive, genitive,
future, n-Deklination, infinitive with zu, connectors and nominalisation at B2.

## 4. Syllabus A2, B1, B2 for English (for German speakers)
Same format. Based on Cambridge Vocabulary Profile and CEFR. Cover at least: past simple and continuous,
present perfect versus past simple (a German-speaker problem), future forms, comparatives, modals, first and
second conditionals, passive, relative clauses, reported speech, gerund versus infinitive, phrasal verbs,
third conditional and linking devices at B2, plus false friends relevant at each level.

## 5. Reading and listening content by level
What text length and type each exam level uses (words per text, emails, notices, articles). Recommend
what authored passages per unit we need, with lengths and question types that can be auto-marked.

## 6. A practice exam design
Recommend, for a client-only site, a practice exam for each level, using auto-markable tasks modelled on
the real exams' skill areas but with our own wording: sections, number of items, timing, pass threshold.
How to handle writing and speaking honestly with no human marker (for example a model answer plus a
self-check list). Exact wording we should show so nobody thinks a pass here is an official certificate or
predicts a real result.

## 7. Recommendations
A short list of the decisions you would make, with vocabulary size targets per level.
